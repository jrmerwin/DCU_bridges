## 1. What each observer can know

For anchor b, the initial view is Anc(b). After the observation checkpoint, define

    V_b(t) = Anc(b) union [union Anc(z) over observed births z with b in parents(z)].

The source additionally knows the emission child m because it participated in its formation immediately before the observation window. The initial source frame contains both receiver anchors, so the return certificate can be recognized without consulting an outside list of target identities.

These are growing ancestry-closed *unions*, not an assertion that the same immutable apex acquires new ancestors. A frame permanently fixed to Anc(b) cannot acquire an object born after b: that is an exact consequence of immutable parentage.

Each local event log contains only its local maintenance index, own newborn-child handles, encountered partner handles, and newly available parent cards. Object handles are reassigned consecutively *inside each observer*. They do not disclose global object IDs, iteration numbers, n, F, random-generator state, or unrelated births. Global-to-local crosswalks and full histories are auditor data, separate from the private log.

The full participating ancestry is available under the added readout convention. This is not a proof of physically free copying, a gate compiler, or instantaneous travel across physical distance. At a receiving construction the input factors are recorded by the source definition; the newborn itself remains unrecorded until used. Outstanding workload need not be zero. The coarse global work pool does not yet define which completion time makes a particular physical message readable.

## 2. A pulse, a receipt, and a return certificate

The pulse is an actual new comparison m={A,x} from Cell29. A is the first parent in its saved canonical parent pair. Readers B=3 (primary) and B=4 (transfer) were excluded from every emission event.

A pulse carrier is any actually present object c with m in Anc(c). The reader receives pulse ancestry when a new child r={B,c} forms. Merely discovering r in an omniscient simulation is not enough: it must appear through B's own event log. Any such r is a valid receipt.

A return is a later new child w={A,d} where d contains at least one valid B-receipt in its ancestry. A can then reconstruct the finite certificate

    m in Anc(r), B in parents(r), r in Anc(w), A in parents(w).

Every parent is pre-burst. Thus the receipt and its return cannot be sibling constructions in the same batch. A single source clock counts its own services between emission and return; no synchronization of different clocks or remote timestamps is needed to define that count.

Importantly, a source cannot know which remote receipt was first. The implemented return accepts **any** valid receipt, not a simulator-selected first receipt inaccessible to the source. During development the first-receipt-only draft was replaced with this locally recognizable rule. This corrects an observability limitation; it does not select a mass, rate, or favorable outcome. In the released finite panel the 12 return counts are unchanged. All actual receipt roots remain in the result.

The use of the word pulse refers to tagged *provenance*. The tag is a computational shortcut equal to actual ancestor membership, verified at the end of every history. No marker affects selection or workload, and no sender-controlled payload or timestamp encoder has been compiled. The return is evidence of an actual receiver contact, not a consciously generated acknowledgement from an independently implemented agent.

## 3. Exact local-time contact law

Let R=F+n and q=min(Gamma,R). An individual token has p=q/R. Suppose a reader does not yet know the pulse, and C current objects carry it. The conditional probability of first receipt on that reader's own service is

    h(R,q,C) = 1 - choose(R-1-C,q-1)/choose(R-1,q-1).

Conditioning on the reader being among the q selected tokens leaves a uniformly selected q-1-subset from the other R-1 requests. Failure means selecting no carrier.

Before first receipt, every reader/carrier parent pair is absent: if one had formed earlier it would already have produced receipt in the observer's log. Thus this hypergeometric event is exactly the first-receipt event, not just a necessary condition. The same argument applies to a source awaiting any returned receipt, with C equal to its current receipt-carrier count.

The per-iteration hazard is p*h. With one newly born carrier and Gamma=3:

    h=2/(R-1),      p*h=6/[R(R-1)].

This distinction is central. A clock service is a one-token event. A contact is a joint event. Even relative to the receiver's own service ticks, contact availability depends on load. That does NOT imply different mean intrinsic rates for identical clocks at fixed anchors. It is a distinction between process types under a shared capacity rule, not a gravitational redshift.

The exact formula depends on the number of carriers and the total pool, not their parent–child distances from the receiver. Those distances range from two to six across the starting-state audit. Delayed acquisition comes from waiting for actual native contact; no finite spatial-speed bound is supplied by this protocol.

The activity factors depend on the evolving graph and backlog. Do not replace the process by independent identical trials, invert an average hazard to claim a mean waiting time, or condition on a future queue trajectory as though it were externally imposed.

For a first-receipt indicator D at a fixed horizon, the stopped hazard sum A=sum 1_pending * p*h satisfies E[D-A]=0. The script retains the Monte Carlo residual and the predictable Bernoulli variance sum. A realized sequence of hazards is not treated as an unconditional survival distribution.

## 7. Interpretation and next gap

The result is an event-local ancestry-access model with finite causal certificates and clock counts. Merely fixing a reader to the ancestry of an immutable old apex freezes its view forever; an updating view is indispensable for observing later constructions. Our updating union is an explicit operational choice.

The result establishes neither physical spatial neighborhoods nor a preferred spatial dimension, photon velocity, gravitational redshift, packet-completion time, or fully compiled observer. Independent regional pools would change the counting law and are not introduced. Incoming comparisons may involve arbitrarily remote graph objects, so the construction is event-local in access, not geometrically local in dynamics.

A useful subsequent branch is a legally updated descendant-carrier observer, compared with these fixed anchors under the same global service law. Its choice of continuation through a multi-child burst must be specified and tested as another observer rule, not inserted as a preferred spacetime trajectory.
